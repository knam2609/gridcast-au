## D4 price level: the per-slot scale decides the ranking

D4 scores |z| of x = asinh(price / $10) against the same half-hour's median and robust scale over the previous 28 days (floor 0.25). The transform is linear for |price| below about $10 and logarithmic above, so a routine move from −$50 to +$50 is 4.62 units of x, about as large as the 95-fold spike from $185 to $17,500 (4.55). Slots whose prices straddle $0 therefore get wide robust scales, and stable slots sit at the floor.

## D6 Isolation Forest: saturation and month-to-month drift

**Drift (A1).** The share of a month's scored intervals at p ≤ 0.001 should be about 0.1% if the month resembles its training window.

**Saturation (A3).** An isolation tree splits between a node's minimum and maximum, so a value beyond the training range is isolated no faster than the training extreme. A disturbance in one feature therefore scores no higher than an ordinary row with that feature at its training extreme. ## D1 demand level: limited by real demand variability

D1 scores |z| of demand against the same half-hour's median and robust scale over the previous 28 days of the same day type. The scale is wide where weather moves demand by thousands of MW from day to day, and the trailing 26 weeks' tail is set by heatwaves and cold snaps, so a fixed 2,000 MW step is unusual only at stable slots. This is the detector working as designed: D2, which conditions on the weather forecast, catches far smaller steps.
